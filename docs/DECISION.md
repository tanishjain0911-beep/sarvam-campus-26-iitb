# Why Suno

Selected idea: #18, Text-to-Speech Content Reader, from the participant's Idea Library.

The selection balances a short build time, a clear demo, broad everyday use, and a manageable correctness burden. It is a judgment about MVP fit, not a measured estimate of population reach.

| Candidate | Benefit | Time-box tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| #18 Content reader | Helps people hear ordinary notices, study notes and messages; language choice and readable text support different preferences | Two well-defined provider endpoints; no account, records database, avatar or retrieval corpus required |
| #6 Citizen services / #45 Scheme journey | Potentially valuable for access to public benefits | Needs current official source coverage, eligibility validation and careful application workflows |
| #31 Bhasha doubt solver | Helpful for students | Requires pedagogical quality checks and a narrower educational audience |
| #46 Grievance drafter | Helps citizens express complaints | Department routing and form/process correctness add implementation and verification work |
| #5 Avatar tutor | Engaging learning experience | Avatar, visual teaching and progress tracking substantially increase the MVP surface |

Suno's initial flow is deliberately small: paste text, choose languages, generate, listen or download. Larger text, high contrast, visible labels, native controls and a text fallback matter more here than feature count. It has not been tested with assistive-technology users or audited for standards compliance.

Future candidates after the hackathon: OCR for photographed notices, native-language interface labels, and longer-text batching with an explicit cost preview. They are outside this MVP.
